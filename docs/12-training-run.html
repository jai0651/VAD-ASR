12.1The one-paragraph version

We are training a 28.0M-parameter Conformer speech recognizer on LibriSpeech train-clean-100 (100 hours of read English), from scratch, on a rented RTX A4000. It maps a waveform to text through two heads trained jointly: a CTC head that learns the alignment, and an attention decoder that learns the language. The target is a word error rate in the low teens; the reference implementation of this exact recipe reaches 8.4%.

12.2The journey of one utterance

Ten seconds of speech, traced through every transformation. Shapes below are printed from the real model, not sketched.

waveform, 16 kHz mono (160000,) float32 │ framing: 25 ms window, 10 ms hop ▼ |rFFT|², 80 triangular mel filters, log log-mel spectrogram (998, 80) 100 frames/s │ CMVN: subtract mean, divide by std, per utterance │ SpecAugment: mask 2 freq bands, 5 time spans ▼ model input (1, 998, 80) │ 2 × Conv2d(3×3, stride 2) → ×4 downsample in time AND frequency ▼ flatten freq into channels → Linear → d_model subsampled sequence (1, 248, 256) 25 frames/s, 40 ms each │ │ 12 × Conformer block (½FFN → self-attention → conv → ½FFN → LayerNorm) ▼ encoded sequence (1, 248, 256) │ ├─▶ CTC head: Linear(256 → 1024) (1, 248, 1024) per-frame subword scores │ └─▶ attention decoder: 6 transformer layers, cross-attends the encoder emits subwords one at a time
The number that governs everything downstream ×4 subsampling. 998 input frames become 248. Three consequences: self-attention is O(T²) so this is a 16× reduction in attention cost; each output frame now represents 40 ms, which sets the finest possible alignment granularity; and the CTC head must fit every subword of the transcript into those 248 slots. That last one is a hard constraint — see §12.5.

12.35The architecture in equations

Everything above, written down precisely. Notation: \(x\) is the waveform, \(T\) the number of input frames, \(T'=T/4\) the encoder frames, \(d=256\) the model dimension, \(h=4\) heads, \(d_k=d/h=64\).

Front end — waveform to log-mel
\[ X[k,t] \;=\; \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} w[n]\,x[tH+n]\,e^{-2\pi j kn/N}, \qquad N=512,\; H=160,\; w=\text{Hann}(400) \] \[ \operatorname{mel}(f)=2595\log_{10}\!\Big(1+\tfrac{f}{700}\Big), \qquad m_{b,t}=\log\Big(\textstyle\sum_{k} \mathcal{H}_{b,k}\,\big|X[k,t]\big|^{2}+\varepsilon\Big) \]
Per-utterance CMVN
\[ \hat m_{b,t}=\frac{m_{b,t}-\mu_b}{\sigma_b+\varepsilon}, \qquad \mu_b=\tfrac1T\sum_t m_{b,t},\quad \sigma_b^2=\tfrac1T\sum_t (m_{b,t}-\mu_b)^2 \]
stepwhy it is there / what breaks without it
25 ms frames, 10 ms hop Speech is wildly non-stationary over a word but roughly stationary over ~25 ms — which is the window in which a Fourier transform is meaningful at all. The 10 ms overlap means an event straddling a frame boundary is never missed.
Hann window \(w[n]\) Cutting a sinusoid at an arbitrary boundary creates a discontinuity, and a discontinuity has energy at every frequency. Without tapering, that spectral leakage smears one clean formant across the whole spectrum.
\(|X|^2\) — discarding phase Phoneme identity lives in the spectral envelope, not the phase, so discarding phase costs recognition almost nothing and halves the input. It is not free everywhere: this is exactly why TTS needs a vocoder to invent phase back.
80 mel filters \(\mathcal{H}\) Two jobs at once. It warps frequency to match cochlear resolution (fine below 1 kHz, coarse above), and it compresses 257 FFT bins into 80 — deliberately blurring pitch harmonics, which vary by speaker, while preserving formants, which carry the phoneme.
\(\log(\cdot)\) Also two jobs. It compresses a ~120 dB dynamic range into a range a network can use, and — the subtle one — it converts multiplicative channel effects (microphone gain, room response) into additive offsets. That is the entire reason the next step can be a simple subtraction.
CMVN The log turned "this mic is 6 dB hotter" into a constant added to every frame of band \(b\); subtracting the per-band mean removes it exactly, so a different microphone lands in the same place in feature space. Skip it and the model burns capacity learning to subtract a per-recording constant, and training is measurably less stable.
Convolutional subsampling (×4) and its length map
\[ T' \;=\; \Big\lfloor \tfrac{\lfloor (T-1)/2\rfloor-1}{2} \Big\rfloor \qquad\text{(two }3\times3\text{ convolutions, stride 2, no padding)} \]

Why: three distinct payoffs from one cheap module. (i) Cost — self-attention is \(O(T^2)\), so \(4\times\) fewer frames is a 16× reduction in attention compute; this is what makes attention affordable on audio at all. (ii) Redundancy — adjacent 10 ms frames are nearly identical, so little information is lost. (iii) Inductive bias — a 2-D convolution over the (time, frequency) image extracts local, shift-invariant patterns such as formant transitions before attention ever runs, which is the job convolutions do better than attention.
The price: 40 ms becomes the finest alignment CTC can express, and \(T'\) is now the hard budget every subword must fit inside (§12.4).

Conformer block — macaron sandwich
\[ \tilde{x} = x + \tfrac12\,\mathrm{FFN}(x) \] \[ x' = \tilde{x} + \mathrm{MHSA}\big(\mathrm{LN}(\tilde{x})\big) \] \[ x'' = x' + \mathrm{Conv}(x') \] \[ y = \mathrm{LN}\Big(x'' + \tfrac12\,\mathrm{FFN}(x'')\Big) \]

Why this shape. The block is deliberately a sandwich: transform → mix globally → mix locally → transform. Each piece does something the others cannot.

sub-modulewhat it contributeswhat it cannot do alone
½·FFN (×2, macaron) Position-wise non-linear capacity — re-mixing the 256 channels at each frame independently. Two half-weighted FFNs around the attention empirically beat one full-weight FFN after it; that sandwich is where "macaron" comes from. This is also where two-thirds of the block's parameters live. Cannot move information between time steps at all.
MHSA (relative position) Global context in one hop: frame 5 can attend directly to frame 200. Essential for long-range agreement — the same word said twice, or a speaker's vowel space established seconds earlier. Weak at short, precise, translation-invariant patterns; a softmax over 248 frames is a blunt tool for "was there a burst 20 ms ago?"
Conv module (depthwise k=31) Exactly that short-range job: formant transitions, plosive bursts, the fine time structure that identifies a phone. 1.24 s of receptive field, at \(O(C\cdot k)\) cost instead of \(O(T^2)\). Cannot reach beyond its kernel, however deep you stack it cheaply.
Residuals + final LayerNorm Keeps a short gradient path through 12 blocks and keeps activation scale bounded, so the stack trains without exotic initialisation. —

The headline claim of Conformer is precisely this pairing: attention and convolution are complementary, not competing, so use both in every block rather than choosing.

Relative-position self-attention (Transformer-XL form)
\[ e_{ij}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_k}}\Big[\; \underbrace{(q_i+u)^{\!\top} k_j}_{\text{content} \,\times\, \text{content}} \;+\; \underbrace{(q_i+v)^{\!\top} W_p\, r_{\,i-j}}_{\text{content} \,\times\, \text{relative position}} \;\Big] \] \[ \alpha_{ij}=\frac{M_{ij}\exp(e_{ij})}{\sum_{j'} M_{ij'}\exp(e_{ij'})}, \qquad z_i=\sum_j \alpha_{ij}\,v_j \]

Why each term exists. The first is content×content — "which other frames sound relevant to me". The second is content×position — "which offsets are relevant to me", e.g. a vowel attending to the burst 60 ms before it. Splitting them means the model can learn "look slightly back" as a rule independent of what it is currently hearing.

The two learned vectors \(u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d_k}\) are what remains of the absolute-position terms after the decomposition. \(u\) is a global content bias — which keys matter regardless of the query (silence, say) — and \(v\) a global position bias — which offsets matter regardless of the query. Both are cheap: \(2 \times 64\) numbers per head.

Note what is absent: no position×position term, and no absolute index anywhere. The score depends only on \(i-j\), "40 ms earlier", never "frame 412". That is exactly why a model trained on 10 s utterances still works on 30 s ones — an absolute-position model would be extrapolating into embeddings it never saw during training.

Dynamic chunk mask — one model, offline and streaming
\[ M_{ij}=\mathbb{1}\Big[\big\lfloor\tfrac{j}{C}\big\rfloor \le \big\lfloor\tfrac{i}{C}\big\rfloor\Big] \cdot \mathbb{1}\Big[\big\lfloor\tfrac{i}{C}\big\rfloor-\big\lfloor\tfrac{j}{C}\big\rfloor \le L\Big] \]

\(C\) is the chunk width in encoder frames (\(C=16\Rightarrow 640\) ms of lookahead) and \(L\) the number of history chunks; \(C\!=\!0\) means full context. Resampling \(C\) every batch during training is what makes one set of weights work at any latency.

Convolution module — the local half
\[ \mathrm{Conv}(x)=\mathrm{Drop}\Big(W_{p_2}*\;\mathrm{Swish}\big(\mathrm{BN}(\mathrm{DWConv}_{31}(\;\mathrm{GLU}(W_{p_1}*\mathrm{LN}(x))\;))\big)\Big) \] \[ \mathrm{GLU}(a,b)=a\odot\sigma(b),\qquad \mathrm{Swish}(x)=x\,\sigma(x) \]

Why each piece:

  • Pointwise conv → GLU. \(W_{p_1}\) expands to \(2d\) channels, then GLU splits them in half and uses one half to gate the other. That is a multiplicative interaction — "let this feature through only if that other feature is present" — which a plain linear layer cannot express, and it costs one extra \(1{\times}1\) convolution.
  • Depthwise convolution, kernel 31. "Depthwise" means one filter per channel rather than a full \(C\times C\) mixing, so cost is \(O(C\cdot k)\) instead of \(O(C^2\cdot k)\) — roughly 256× cheaper here, which is what lets the kernel be as wide as 31. That width is \(31 \times 40\,\)ms = 1.24 s of receptive field: syllable scale, precisely the range attention handles poorly.
  • BatchNorm → Swish. Normalisation keeps the pre-activation scale stable across a deep stack; Swish \(x\sigma(x)\) is smooth and non-monotonic, and empirically outperforms ReLU here — a small negative response is apparently useful rather than something to clip away.
  • Causal (left-only) padding. For streaming this is load-bearing: pad symmetrically and the module quietly reads ~15 frames of future, which would make the chunk mask a fiction and every latency number a lie. A test asserts this by re-encoding a prefix and requiring bit-identical output.
Decoder layer (pre-norm) — one of 6
\[ g = g + \mathrm{SelfAttn}\big(\mathrm{LN}(g);\,M^{\text{causal}}\big), \qquad M^{\text{causal}}_{uv}=\mathbb{1}[\,v \le u\,] \] \[ g = g + \mathrm{CrossAttn}\big(\mathrm{LN}(g),\; \underbrace{z}_{\text{encoder out}}\big) \] \[ g = g + \mathrm{FFN}\big(\mathrm{LN}(g)\big) \]

Why these three, in this order. Self-attention over the tokens emitted so far is what makes the decoder a language model — it is the only place \(y_{<u}\) enters. The causal mask \(M^{\text{causal}}\) is not optional: without it token \(u\) can see token \(u{+}1\), the loss collapses to near zero, and WER stays at 100% — a failure that looks like spectacular training. Cross-attention is the only place the decoder touches the audio, and it is unmasked because the whole utterance is available. The FFN plays the same position-wise role as in the encoder.

Why pre-norm (LayerNorm inside the residual rather than after it): post-norm transformers need careful warmup and often diverge without it. Pre-norm keeps a clean identity path from input to output, so the stack trains stably — and given §12.6 is a story about a warmup mistake, having one less thing depending on warmup is worth the small quality trade.

12.4The loss function, in full

The objective
\[ \mathcal{L} \;=\; \lambda\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CTC}} \;+\; (1-\lambda)\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{att}}, \qquad \lambda = 0.3 \]

Term 1 — CTC: learning where things are said

We have 248 encoder frames and, say, 30 subwords. Nobody told us which frames correspond to which subword. CTC solves this by introducing a blank symbol and defining a many-to-one collapse:

frame labels:  _ _ h h _ e l l _ l o _ _      (_ = blank)
  merge repeats:      _ h _ e l _ l o _
  drop blanks:          h   e l   l o          -> "hello"

Let \(\mathcal{B}\) be that collapse map, \(V\) the subword vocabulary plus blank \(\varnothing\), and \(y\) the target of length \(U\). The model emits a distribution over \(V\) at each of the \(T'\) encoder frames. The probability of the target is the sum over every frame labelling that collapses to it:

CTC objective
\[ P(y\mid x)=\sum_{\pi\,\in\,\mathcal{B}^{-1}(y)}\;\prod_{t=1}^{T'} P(\pi_t\mid x), \qquad \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CTC}}=-\frac{1}{U}\log P(y\mid x) \]

That sum has exponentially many terms, so it is computed by a forward-backward recursion over the extended target \(y'\) — the target with blanks interleaved, \(y'=(\varnothing,y_1,\varnothing,y_2,\dots,y_U,\varnothing)\), of length \(2U{+}1\):

Forward recursion
\[ \alpha_t(s)=P(\pi_t=y'_s\mid x)\cdot \begin{cases} \alpha_{t-1}(s)+\alpha_{t-1}(s-1), & y'_s=\varnothing \;\text{ or }\; y'_s=y'_{s-2}\\[4pt] \alpha_{t-1}(s)+\alpha_{t-1}(s-1)+\alpha_{t-1}(s-2), & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \] \[ P(y\mid x)=\alpha_{T'}(2U{+}1)+\alpha_{T'}(2U) \]

The two-case split is the blank's purpose. The three-term branch lets the path skip a blank and move straight to the next label; the two-term branch forbids that skip when the next label repeats the previous one, forcing a blank between them. Without it, "hello" and "helo" would be indistinguishable.

Feasibility — the condition zero_infinity hides
\[ T' \;\ge\; U + r, \qquad r=\big|\{\,u:\;y_u=y_{u+1}\,\}\big| \]

If this fails, \(P(y\mid x)=0\) and the loss is \(+\infty\); zero_infinity=True replaces it with a zero gradient rather than an error, so those utterances silently stop contributing. Speed perturbation at \(1.1\times\) shrinks \(T'\) by 10% without changing \(U\), which is why the preflight gate checks this at the fastest speed and counts repeats.

Why the loss value looks wrong at initialisation With reduction="mean" PyTorch divides by \(U\), the target length — while the path probability accumulates over all \(T'\) frames. For a uniform model, \(P(\pi_t)=1/|V|\) at every frame, so \[ \mathcal{L}^{(0)}_{\mathrm{CTC}} \;\approx\; \frac{T'}{U}\,\ln|V| \] With \(T'/U\approx 6\) and \(|V|=1024\) that is \(\approx 41\) — and we measured 33–35. Expecting \(\ln|V|=6.93\) makes a perfectly healthy model look broken. This exact error was in our own validation script.
Two facts about this number that mislead you (a) reduction="mean" divides by TARGET length, not frame count. So an untrained model's CTC loss is roughly (frames/token) × ln(V), not ln(V). With ~6 frames per token and V=1024 that is ≈ 41, and we measured 33–35 at init. Reading it as "should start near ln(1024)=6.9" makes a healthy model look broken — a mistake that was in this repo's own validation script until it was corrected.

(b) zero_infinity=True silently drops impossible samples. If an utterance has more target tokens than encoder frames, CTC is infeasible; rather than erroring, that sample contributes a zero gradient. Choose a bad vocabulary and you quietly train on a fraction of your data with no warning. The preflight gate now checks this explicitly.

Term 2 — attention: learning what is said

CTC's tractability comes from a strong assumption: conditional independence — given the audio, each frame's label is predicted without reference to its neighbours. That is exactly why a CTC-only model can emit orthographically impossible text — formally, CTC factorises as

\[ P_{\mathrm{CTC}}(\pi\mid x)=\prod_{t} P(\pi_t\mid x) \qquad\text{— no } \pi_{<t} \text{ on the right-hand side.} \]

The decoder removes that assumption by conditioning on its own history:

Attention objective, with label smoothing
\[ P_{\mathrm{att}}(y\mid x)=\prod_{u=1}^{U+1} P\big(y_u \,\big|\, y_{<u},\,x\big) \] \[ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{att}} = -\frac{1}{U+1}\sum_{u=1}^{U+1}\sum_{c\in V} \tilde q_u(c)\, \log P\big(y_u = c \mid y_{<u}, x\big) \] \[ \tilde q_u(c) = (1-\epsilon)\,\delta_{c,\,y_u} + \frac{\epsilon}{|V|}, \qquad \epsilon = 0.1 \]

Targets are wrapped as \(y_{\text{in}}=(\texttt{sos},y_1,\dots,y_U)\) and \(y_{\text{out}}=(y_1,\dots,y_U,\texttt{eos})\) with one shared sos/eos token, so the \(U{+}1\)-th step is "predict that the utterance is over" — stopping is learned, not a heuristic. Padding positions use \(\varnothing\) as the ignore index, which is unambiguous because blank can never be an attention target.

Decoding — CTC proposes, the decoder rescores
\[ \hat y=\arg\max_{y\,\in\,\mathcal{N}(x)}\Big[\, \lambda_d \log P_{\mathrm{CTC}}(y\mid x) +(1-\lambda_d)\log P_{\mathrm{att}}(y\mid x)\Big], \qquad \lambda_d=0.5 \]

\(\mathcal{N}(x)\) is the n-best list from CTC prefix beam search. Because the decoder only scores a fixed candidate set, it runs once per hypothesis in a single batched forward — far cheaper than an autoregressive beam search, which would evaluate it once per output token per beam.

Why both, on one encoder Each head fixes the other's failure mode. CTC is monotonic by construction, which stops the attention from wandering or looping — the same failure Module 4's TTS needed guided attention to avoid. The decoder supplies the implicit language model CTC structurally cannot have. One encoder pays for both, and at decode time the same split is reused: CTC beam search proposes an n-best list, the decoder rescores it in a single batched forward.

12.5The methodology around the loss

Batching — by frames, not by count

Utterances vary from 1 s to 16 s. Batching by count means one 16 s clip forces twelve 2 s clips to be padded to 16 s, and most of the GPU computes zeros. We bucket by length and fill each batch to a frame budget, so batches are length-homogeneous and padding is small. The cost of a batch is batch_size × longest, which is what the budget caps.

Augmentation — three levels, and when each switches on

levelwhatwhy
waveformspeed perturb 0.9× / 1.0× / 1.1×triples the corpus; shifts pitch too, so it is really speaker augmentation
waveformnoise mixing at 5–20 dB SNRa model that has only heard studio speech collapses on a phone call
featuresSpecAugment: 2 frequency masks (≤27 bins), 5 time masks (≤5% each)forces inference from partial evidence; the highest-leverage line in any small-data recipe

Augmentation is delayed until the alignment forms (AUG_START). Measured on this project: with SpecAugment on from step 0, CTC reached 1.06 by step 400 on a small subset; with it off, 0.13. Regularising a model that hasn't learned the task yet only lengthens the plateau.

A subtlety that invalidates a naive feasibility check Speed perturbation at 1.1× shortens the audio by 10% but leaves the transcript unchanged — so the frames-per-token ratio the model actually trains on is worse than the one you compute from raw durations. And CTC needs a blank between adjacent repeated tokens, so the true requirement is frames ≥ tokens + repeats. Validating on raw durations without repeats flatters you on both counts.

Learning-rate schedule — the part we got wrong

Ours: linear warmup, then cosine decay to a floor
\[ \eta(s)=\eta_{\max}\cdot \begin{cases} \dfrac{s}{S_w}, & s < S_w \\[10pt] \phi+(1-\phi)\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}\left(1+\cos\pi\dfrac{s-S_w}{S-S_w}\right), & s \ge S_w \end{cases} \] \[ \eta_{\max}=10^{-3},\quad \phi=0.05,\quad S=15000,\quad S_w=\underbrace{1500}_{\text{what we used}}\;\text{vs}\;\underbrace{15000}_{\text{reference}} \]

For comparison, ESPnet and WeNet use the inverse-square-root (Noam) form, which never fully decays and warms up far more slowly:

Reference: Noam / warmuplr
\[ \eta(s)=\eta_{\max}\cdot \sqrt{S_w}\cdot\min\!\big(s^{-1/2},\; s\,S_w^{-3/2}\big) \]

Warmup is not decoration. At step 0 the attention weights are near-uniform, so \(\partial \mathcal{L}/\partial e_{ij}\) is large and badly conditioned; a full-size step there reliably lands the model in a bad basin. With \(S_w=1500\) our learning rate hit its peak at step 1,500 — and the CTC loss went flat at step ≈1,200 and never recovered. The literature names this exact mechanism (§12.6).

12.6What we watch, and the failure we hit

Three signals, in order of how early they tell you something:

signalhealthywhat it caught
blank-frame fraction
share of encoder frames whose CTC argmax is blank		rises to ~0.85–0.95, then comes downpinned at 1.0000 — the model had stopped emitting labels entirely
CTC lossfalls, plateaus, then falls off a cliffflat at 5.88 for 2,000 steps

CTC blank collapse — what actually happened The model discovered that emitting <blank> on every frame is a safe, finite-loss answer, and converged into it: 100% of frames decoding to blank, mean P(blank) 0.89, output entropy 0.82 nats out of a possible 6.93, and greedy decoding returning the empty string. It does not recover on its own.

This is invisible in the loss (which just goes flat at a plausible-looking value) and invisible in WER (which pins at 1.0 — indistinguishable from "still early"). That is why blank-frame fraction is now logged at every evaluation, with an explicit stop message.

The diagnosis, from the literature rather than from guessing

"Why does CTC result in peaky behavior?" identifies blank-dominated convergence as a property of local convergence, and names the aggravating conditions — chief among them aggressive learning rates during early training, with learning-rate warmup as the first listed mitigation.

Our warmup was 1,500 steps. Every reference recipe uses 15,000–35,000. The learning rate reached its peak at step 1,500; CTC went flat at step ~1,200 and never recovered.
