Beating Smart Turn Where It's Weak

A build plan for a telephony-native end-of-turn detector: why the gap is real, the trap that would invalidate the whole thing, the augmentation pipeline, and the benchmark that is the actual contribution.

← hub · state of the art

9.1Verdict

Do it Of everything in §8, this is the one project where a single person with an M4 can plausibly produce a model that beats the best open model on a defensible benchmark. Four properties make it winnable, and all four have to hold — they rarely do:
  • The baseline is small. Smart Turn v3 is ~8M parameters (a Whisper-tiny encoder plus a linear head). You are not competing with 600M.
  • The baseline is fully open. BSD-2 weights, open training code, open train/test datasets on HuggingFace. You can reproduce its number before you try to beat it — which means your claim is checkable.
  • The gap is narrow and nameable. Not "better turn detection" but "8 kHz μ-law, tandem-transcoded, Indian-English/Hindi code-switched".
  • It's the layer with the most product leverage and the fewest papers. Turn-taking is what makes an agent feel human, and this repo's own endpointing.py is still a fixed 700 ms timeout.

9.2Is the gap real? Yes, and it's documented

Not a hunch. Pipecat issue #3844, filed February 2026 and still open:

  • LocalSmartTurnAnalyzerV3 builds a WhisperFeatureExtractor with sampling_rate=16000 and does no internal resampling. Twilio's own docs tell you to set audio_in_sample_rate=8000.
  • Result: the model interprets speech at 2× speed with shifted pitch, and fails silently — no error, no warning.
  • Measured by the reporter: 6 of 20 utterances flipped Complete ↔ Incomplete; max probability shift 0.94; mean turn duration in production dropped 51% (2.33 s → 1.14 s); phone numbers fragmented across turns because digit sequences stopped being recognised as incomplete.

Phone numbers fragmenting mid-utterance is not a benchmark abstraction. That is a broken IVR replacement, in production, today.

9.3The trap that would sink the project

Read this before writing any code That bug is an integration bug, not proof of a capability gap. Resample 8 kHz → 16 kHz before the feature extractor and the reported failure disappears. If your benchmark compares your model against naively-fed-8 kHz Smart Turn, you will "win" by 30 points, someone will point out the one-line fix, and the entire project is dismissed.

You must publish three baselines, not one:
  1. Smart Turn, naive 8 kHz — reproduces the bug. Interesting as a field-safety finding, worthless as a model comparison.
  2. Smart Turn, correctly resampled 8→16 kHz — this is the real baseline. Every claim you make must be against this number.
  3. Smart Turn, original wideband audio — the ceiling, showing how much the channel costs anyone.
The durable gap is that even correctly resampled, μ-law-quantised, band-limited, twice-transcoded audio is out-of-distribution for a model trained on clean wideband mic recordings. Upsampling does not restore the 4–8 kHz band — and fricatives, sibilants and the aspiration cues that mark a completed clause live up there. That is your thesis, and it's a defensible one.

9.4What you build

Everything here is buildable from the primitives already in this repo: src/audio/features.py is the front-end, src/vad/model.py is the conv-over-time skeleton, scripts/09 is the hard-case-selection training loop.

8 kHz μ-law call audio │ telephony augmentation (§9.5) — applied at TRAIN time to clean data ▼ framing + log-mel front-end write it yourself first; src/audio/features.py │ 64 mels, fmax 3.8 kHz (not 8 kHz — there is nothing above it) ▼ SpecAugment 2 freq masks, 2 time masks │ ▼ causal conv / Conformer encoder ~4-8M params; NO look-ahead beyond the window │ ▼ binary EOU head + focal loss "does the human expect me to reply now?" │ ▼ ONNX export → int8 target: p95 < 25 ms on one CPU core

Design choices worth defending

  • 8 kHz native, not upsampled. Smart Turn upsamples into a 16 kHz model. Train at 8 kHz with fmax ≈ 3.8 kHz and 64 mels: you spend every mel bin on a band that actually carries information, instead of spending a third of them on the empty 4–8 kHz region. This is the clearest architectural reason you should win on-channel.
  • Causal. An end-of-turn detector that needs future context has added its look-ahead to the response latency. Causal convolutions or left-context-only attention.
  • Focal loss. The classes are wildly imbalanced — most frames are mid-utterance. γ ≈ 2 focuses the gradient on the ambiguous boundary cases, which is exactly where the product lives.
  • Predict at a decision rate, not once per utterance. Smart Turn classifies a trailing window. Emitting P(end-of-turn) every 40–80 ms lets the orchestrator act the instant confidence crosses a threshold, and it makes the model directly droppable into EndpointDetector in place of the fixed timeout.

9.5The telephony augmentation pipeline

This is the part that is genuinely yours, and it is mostly signal processing rather than ML. Take Smart Turn's open training set (clean, wideband) and push it through a real phone network in software:

stagewhat to dowhy it matters
Band-limit + resample16 k → 8 k with a proper anti-alias filter; also model the 300–3400 Hz passbandthe fundamental information loss; everything else is on top
G.711 μ-law round-tripencode/decode; 8-bit logarithmic quantisationadds quantisation noise that is signal-dependent — loudest where speech is loudest
Tandem transcodingμ-law → G.729/Opus-narrowband → μ-lawthe real killer. Carrier → SIP trunk → PBX often means two or three codecs. Artefacts compound non-linearly
Packet loss1–8% loss, bursty (Gilbert-Elliott), with PLC-style concealmentconcealed gaps look like pauses — the single most likely cause of a false "turn ended"
Jitter buffervariable delay, occasional frame repeat/dropchanges local timing, which is exactly what a prosody model reads
Comfort noise / VAD-DTXreplace silence with synthetic CN at the right levelcarriers do this. It means your "silence" is not silence, and a model trained on true silence is surprised
AGC + compressiondynamic-range compression, level normalisationflattens the falling-pitch, falling-energy cue that marks the end of a clause
Line noisehum, hiss, crosstalk, hold music bleed, IVR beeps, DTMFyour hard negatives; the analogue of Module 1b's noise bed

Two rules that decide whether this works:

  1. Augment on the fly, per epoch, with random parameters. The augmentation space is your dataset. A pre-baked degraded copy is one sample from it and the model will overfit the specific artefacts.
  2. Hold out augmentation configurations, not just utterances. Train on μ-law + G.729; test on a codec chain the model never saw. Otherwise you are measuring memorisation of artefacts, and real carriers will differ.

9.6Data and labels

  • Base: the Smart Turn v3.x train sets on HuggingFace. Open licence, already labelled, and using them makes your comparison exactly like-for-like — same content, different channel.
  • Your moat: real call audio from tonecall, with turn boundaries labelled. This is the asset nobody else can download. Get the legal and consent position straight first — recording, retention and publication of call audio are governed by real rules, and a public benchmark built on uncleared call recordings is a liability, not an asset. If publishing audio isn't clearable, publish features and labels, or a synthetic re-recording that reproduces the phenomena.
  • Indic code-switching: IndicVoices (~12k h, 22 languages, conversational and code-switched) is the best open source. It is not telephony-native, so it goes through the same §9.5 pipeline. Note the honest limitation: Smart Turn covers Hindi and Marathi already, so your claim must be about code-switched Indian English over a phone line — a narrower and more truthful claim than "Indian languages".
  • Labelling policy is the hidden hard part. "End of turn" is not objective — a 400 ms pause before "…um, yes" is a completed turn for one annotator and a hesitation for another. Write the policy down, measure inter-annotator agreement on ~200 clips, and publish it. A benchmark whose label definition is unstated is not a benchmark.

9.7The benchmark is the contribution

The model is the artefact people will download; the benchmark is what makes the work citable. It doesn't exist today, which is the opportunity.

Report losses honestly and prominently. "We lose 1.5 points on clean wideband, we win 9 points on transcoded telephony, here is why" is a far stronger artefact than a table where you win everywhere — which nobody believes anyway.

Metrics that reflect the product

  • Not accuracy. The two errors are not symmetric: interrupting a user mid-sentence is much worse than a 200 ms extra wait. Report false-endpoint rate and missed-endpoint rate separately, and publish the full ROC — then pick an operating point and justify it.
  • Time-to-decision, not just correctness: how many ms after the true end of turn does the model fire? A model that's right 400 ms late has cost you the entire latency advantage.
  • p50/p95 CPU latency on a named machine, int8 and fp32, single-threaded. Smart Turn's 12 ms is the number to beat or match.
  • A slice for digit sequences. "My number is four two…" is the failure in issue #3844 and the most commercially important case in telephony.

9.9How this could fail

  • Smart Turn is a moving target. v3.1 added real human audio, v3.2 added café/office noise and fixed short-utterance handling. Assume the clean-audio baseline improves again before you publish. Your advantage must be the channel, not general quality — and the channel is a much slower thing for them to fix, because it needs telephony data they don't have.
  • They ship a fix. The one-line resample fix for #3844 will land. Good: it removes the noise and leaves your real claim standing. Plan for it rather than racing it.
  • Data volume. Degraded copies of one open dataset is one dataset. Without real call audio the result may not transfer. Budget the legal and labelling work as a first-class task, not an afterthought.
  • Label noise dominates. If inter-annotator agreement on your eval set is 85%, a 3-point win is not a win. Measure agreement before you claim anything.

9.10If you want a second and third option

  1. 8 kHz-native denoiser. Same thesis, different stage: GTCRN, DTLN and DeepFilterNet are all wideband. Retrain a GTCRN-class model at 8 kHz on telephony-degraded DNS data. Smaller upside than turn detection, but the cheapest to execute — 23.7K parameters, and denoiser/evaluate.py already computes PESQ/STOI/SI-SDR.
  2. Code-switched Indian-English ASR. Fine-tune Parakeet or Whisper on IndicVoices' code-switched split pushed through §9.5's pipeline. Highest commercial value, lowest novelty — you'd be doing well-understood fine-tuning rather than producing a new artefact.
  3. A telephony voice-agent benchmark suite. The union of the above: one replayable corpus, one harness, per-stage and end-to-end metrics. Least glamorous, and the thing a company in this space would most want to own.

Turn detection is still the pick: it is the only one where the artefact is a model that is state of the art at something, and the benchmark is a by-product you'd have to build anyway.
