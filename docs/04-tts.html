4.1The two-stage decomposition

"the old man's eyes" ~19 characters ▼ acoustic model (learned) src/tts/model.py log-mel spectrogram (T×80) ~250 frames — WHO DECIDES T? ▼ vocoder (here: pure DSP, zero training) src/tts/vocoder.py waveform @16 kHz ~40,000 samples

Why split? The two halves are different problems. Text→mel is a sequence problem (durations, prosody, pronunciation). Mel→waveform is a signal problem (invert a lossy transform). Historically they advanced independently — Tacotron improved stage 1 while WaveNet/HiFi-GAN revolutionized stage 2 — and the interface between them is exactly the log-mel you built in Module 0. Modern systems (VITS, StyleTTS2 → Kokoro) fuse the stages end-to-end for quality, but you can't see why that helps until you've felt the seam.

4.2The acoustic model: attention decides time

ASR's alignment problem, inverted. CTC discovered the alignment hidden in the audio; TTS must decide one — how many frames is the "o" in "go"? Tacotron's answer: generate mel frames autoregressively, and at every step use attention over the text to know where you are:

every decoder step (= r mel frames): 1 prenet(previous frame) "what did I just say?" 2 attention over encoder states "which characters am I on?" ← the core 3 GRU state update "remember where I am" 4 project [state, context] → r×80 "paint the next slice of mel" 5 stop head → P(done) "am I finished talking?"

The four tricks that make it trainable (and what happened without them)

TrickWhat it doesObserved in this repo
Teacher forcingduring training, feed the true previous frame, not the model's own output — otherwise early training compounds its own garbagestandard; also the root of the exposure-bias problem in 4.3

4.3The training story (including the failure)

This actually happened in this repo, and it's the most instructive part:

  1. Attempt 1 — 3000 steps, no attention prior. Mel L1 fell smoothly 0.81 → 0.24. Sounds like success — but the loss was lying. L1 falls early just by predicting blurry average speech. The free-running attention map was a smear; Whisper-as-judge heard one half-sentence correctly and nothing else; generation stopped at 2.2 s on an 82-character sentence.
  2. Diagnosis. With 19 training utterances the attention never had enough signal to discover "text is read left to right" on its own. And the loss curve can't tell you: for seq2seq TTS, watch the alignment map, not the loss.
  3. Attempt 2 — guided attention (DC-TTS trick). Add a penalty mean(A·W) where W[t,n] = 1 − exp(−(n/N − t/T)²/2g²): attention mass far from the diagonal is taxed, near it is free. It's a prior, not a constraint — with enough data you'd drop it. Alignment then forms in hundreds of steps instead of never.
  4. Exposure bias, managed. Even aligned, a teacher-forced model drifts when fed its own imperfect frames at inference — attention can stall on one character. We implemented stop-head guards (2 consecutive votes + ~5 frames/char minimum) and measured the popular harder hack — inference-time attention windowing — to be worse (judge CER 0.22 → 0.44 on the best sentence): the model was trained on full-softmax attention, and renormalizing inside a window feeds it context vectors it has never seen. Honest fixes live in training (more data, scheduled sampling) or in architectures that delete autoregression entirely (4.6).
  5. Checkpoint selection by the judge. Free-running quality oscillates between checkpoints even while training loss is flat — so the training script transcribes its own synthesis with Whisper every 1000 steps and keeps the checkpoint with the best CER as tts.pt. "Last" is not "best" for generative models; select on the metric you actually care about.
Transferable lesson Generative models can achieve good training loss while being unusable, because the loss measures frame-level regression, not the property you care about (intelligibility). Always keep an end-to-end judge — we transcribe our own TTS output with Whisper and compare CER against the input text. Production TTS teams do exactly this (ASR-based intelligibility evals) alongside human MOS ratings.

4.4The stop token problem

The model must also decide when to shut up — a binary "am I done?" head per step. Subtle imbalance: padded batches make "done" frames outnumber "still talking" frames for short utterances, and one false positive truncates mid-word. Our guards: require 2 consecutive stop votes AND ~5 frames per input character before stopping. (Kokoro-class models avoid this class of bug entirely by predicting all durations up front — see 4.6.)

4.5Griffin-Lim: recovering what Module 0 destroyed

The mel pipeline threw away two things; the vocoder must reinvent both:

  • Frequency detail — 257 FFT bins were pooled into 80 triangles. Least-squares undo: multiply by the filterbank's pseudo-inverse (torch.linalg.pinv) and clamp negatives.
  • Phase — the power spectrum kept |X|² and discarded every phase angle, but the inverse FFT needs complex numbers. Griffin-Lim (1984) exploits the redundancy of overlapping frames (400-sample frames, 160 hop → 60% overlap): iterate ISTFT → STFT → keep our magnitude, adopt the re-measured phase, and the phases are squeezed toward mutual consistency.

Measured here: spectral convergence 0.235 → 0.112 over 60 iterations; gain-invariant mel MAE 0.22 on voiced frames. Listen to outputs/07_roundtrip.wav: intelligible, but robotic/"phasey" — the phase is consistent, not true. Also note our ISTFT is hand-built overlap-add with the synthesis-window normalization (Σ w² divisor) — the detail everyone gets wrong first.

Why neural vocoders exist That robotic residue is the entire reason for WaveNet (2016, autoregressive sample-by-sample, glacial), WaveRNN, then GAN vocoders — HiFi-GAN (2020) generates the waveform in one pass with a discriminator enforcing "sounds real", runs faster than realtime on CPU, and is still the workhorse inside many production stacks. Biggest single audio-quality jump in TTS history.

4.6From Tacotron to Kokoro: the modern lineage

Trace your model's two pain points through the table: the stop/stall problems die with explicit durations (FastSpeech), and the Griffin-Lim robotic residue dies with learned vocoders / end-to-end (HiFi-GAN, VITS). Every generation of TTS research is legible as "which of Tacotron's failure modes does this kill?" — which is exactly why building Tacotron first is the right way to learn this field.

4.7Streaming TTS in the pipeline

Both TTS engines yield audio per sentence (synthesize() is a generator): time-to-first-audio ≈ one sentence's synthesis, later sentences render while earlier ones play, and barge-in abandons the generator wasting at most one sentence. Production APIs stream at finer grain (chunked vocoding inside sentences, ~100 ms audio chunks) — same principle, smaller unit. This composes with LLM token streaming: cut the token stream at the first sentence boundary and start TTS while the LLM is still writing.
